# Timetable and attendance

The timetable says who teaches which class where, week after week. The day a
school actually runs differs from it: a teacher is ill, a room floods, a
holiday falls on a Tuesday. Attendance then follows the day, not the plan.
This plan closes the gap between the two.

## What the research settled

- **Registers are legal records in many systems.** England's statutory
  guidance (DfE, Working together to improve school attendance, 2026) is the
  most detailed and is a fair bar for international schools too:
  - a register at the start of each morning session and once each afternoon;
  - the register stays open for the same time each session, at most 30
    minutes; a pupil arriving before it closes is late, after it closes is
    late with no accepted reason (an unauthorised absence);
  - parents report absence, and the school contacts them on the first day
    when no reason came; "reason not yet known" is resolved within 5 school
    days;
  - every amendment keeps the original entry, the new one, the reason, the
    date and who made it;
  - missing 10% of sessions is persistent absence, 50% severe; schools look
    at patterns weekly, by cohort, code, weekday and lesson;
  - parents hear about attendance as time missed, not as percentages;
  - leave is requested in advance by a parent.

  Law and codes differ by country, so thresholds and codes stay per-school
  settings.

- **Cover is daily work.** aSc Substitution, Arbor and Edval Daily all start
  from absent staff, list the lessons that need cover and rank free staff:
  free that period, already teaching the class or subject, fewest covers so
  far (fairness). A lesson may also be cancelled or moved to another room for
  one day. The cover teacher is told, and takes that lesson's register.
- **Timetables carry dates.** A week read by a student or a family shows the
  real days, closures and one-day changes, and moves between weeks. Toddle,
  ManageBac and Arbor all support rotating day cycles (A/B weeks, six-day
  cycles) and more than one bell schedule.

## Steps

1. **Dated weeks.** Every week view carries its dates, marks days the school
   is shut, and moves between weeks, for staff, students and families.
2. **Cover and one-day changes.** Staff absences; a cover board for a day
   listing lessons that need cover with ranked suggestions; cover, cancel or
   move a lesson for one day; the cover teacher is notified, sees the lesson
   on their week and takes its register; students and families see changes.
3. **Absences reported by families.** A parent reports an absence or asks for
   leave in advance, with a note or a document; the office accepts or
   declines; the register shows the reported reason when it is taken.
4. **Lateness and the register window.** Each school sets how long a register
   stays open; arrivals record their minutes late, and after the window the
   mark counts as late after the register closed.
5. **Front office sign-in and sign-out.** Late arrivals and early leavers
   signed in and out at reception, written to the day's registers.
6. **Follow-up.** A first-day call list of unexplained absences with
   guardians' numbers, unresolved reasons past the deadline, persistent and
   severe absence lists, and scheduled reminders for registers not taken.
7. **Corrections.** A correction to a past mark asks for its reason, and the
   register shows each mark's history.
8. **Bell schedules and cycles.** A year's periods, their times and the days
   each runs on are edited from a date on (the year record's Periods tab),
   with bell schedules chosen per day, times per weekday, rotating cycles
   of weeks or days, and timetables dated within the year.
9. **Print and subscribe.** Print any week; subscribe to a personal timetable
   as a calendar feed.
