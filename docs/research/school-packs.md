# School packs: exam boards and privacy law

Researched 2026-10-01 for the per-school packs in
[documents-and-files.md](documents-and-files.md) section 5, step 6: what
Cambridge International, Pearson Edexcel (via JCQ), the IB, UK GDPR, FERPA
and Egypt's personal data protection law require of a school's records,
so a school following any mix of them is held to the strictest rule.
Primary sources were read where they were reachable; gaps are named.

## 1. Exam coursework

**Retention until the board's deadlines.**

- Cambridge International keeps the centre's copy of every candidate's work,
  marks, internal moderation summary, sample copies and forms "until after
  the enquiries about results period", stored securely and not returned
  before. June 2026 series: enquiries close 20 September 2026; November
  2026: 26 February 2027. Appeals run 28 days after an outcome, then 14
  more. (_Cambridge Handbook 2026_ §3.6.4, §6.3, §7.)
- JCQ (UK GCSE and A level; Pearson Edexcel International GCSE) keeps all
  marked work, sampled or not, "until after the deadline for review of
  moderation or until any appeal, malpractice or other results enquiry has
  been completed, whichever is later", backed up, and records the names in
  the moderation sample. (_Instructions for conducting non-examination
  assessments_ 2026-27 §4.8, §6.5; _General Regulations for Approved
  Centres_ 2026-27 §3.25(b).) Pearson's own International A level rules
  were not reachable.
- IB: the school stores all submitted work, internal and external, until
  the session closes, 15 September (May session) or 15 March (November),
  and teachers keep electronic copies of students' past work for three
  years for plagiarism checks. Forms kept at school may be asked for at the
  five-yearly evaluation. (_DP Assessment procedures 2025_ B1 Art. 2.6,
  C3.2.1; _Academic integrity policy_ 2026 p.13.) The MYP procedures were
  not reachable.

**Declarations.**

- JCQ: every candidate signs a declaration after finishing and before
  handing in (own work, sources referenced, AI use acknowledged, kept
  confidential, done under the conditions); the teacher signs that steps
  were taken, help recorded, marking done to the scheme and not by AI
  alone. Electronic signatures count. Kept until results enquiries close.
  Unauthenticated work scores 0. (NEA Instructions §4.6, Appendix 4.)
- Cambridge: one centre declaration on submission, on behalf of candidate
  and teacher; work done with a private tutor cannot be authenticated.
  (Handbook §3.2.1, §5.6.4.)
- IB: student and teacher confirm authenticity at upload (or the school
  holds a signed candidate consent form); the extended essay supervisor
  states hours spent; the EE reflection form and TOK planning form are
  compulsory. (DPAP C5.2.1, D7, D8.4.3.)

**AI.**

- JCQ: students name the tool and date, keep their prompts and the output
  in a non-editable form (such as screenshots) with a short explanation,
  and hand them in with the work; markers record how they treated
  acknowledged AI. (_AI Use in Assessments_, rev. 2, April 2025, §5-6.)
- Cambridge: all generative AI use acknowledged, with prompts, tool, version
  and date; quotations around 50 words at most. (Handbook §3.2.1 and its
  AI guidance page.)
- IB: AI is not banned; uncredited AI is misconduct; citations name the
  prompt and date. Detectors are not evidence on their own. (Academic
  integrity policy, Appendix 6.)

## 2. Requests for records

|                                     | Deadline                  | Notes                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| UK GDPR subject access              | one month, +2 for complex | clock starts when ID or fee is in; holidays do not stop it (Art. 12A, from 5 Feb 2026) |
| England maintained schools, parents | 15 school days            | educational record; separate right                                                     |
| FERPA inspection                    | 45 days                   | parents; the student from 18 (34 CFR 99.10)                                            |
| Egypt, Law 151/2020                 | 6 working days            | complaints decided in 30 working days (Art. 32-33)                                     |

FERPA also requires a **record of disclosures** of personally identifiable
information, kept as long as the record (99.32), and a **directory
information** notice with an opt-out (99.37). It applies only to schools
receiving US Department of Education funds; most private schools abroad do
not.

## 3. Retention and deletion

- UK (DfE guidance): secondary pupil record to the 25th birthday;
  admissions 6 years; attendance registers 6 years; child protection to
  the 25th (75th for sexual abuse). Destruction is authorised by a senior
  leader and logged (what, how many, who). The IRMS toolkit was not
  readable.
- Egypt: data goes when its purpose ends and the data subject is told;
  controllers keep records of erasure requests and proof of erasure;
  processors (Farida) record the date and time of erasure. (Executive
  Regulations Art. 3-4.) Retention is the controller's choice, so it must
  be the school's setting, not ours.
- FERPA sets no retention period; nothing may be destroyed while a request
  to inspect it is pending.

## 4. Children, consent and photos

- **Egypt**: all children's data is sensitive (Law Art. 1). Under 15,
  explicit written guardian consent, stating how long it lasts, before
  collection; 15-18, the guardian's consent through the child or guardian.
  Children's data may not be used for classification, tracking or
  behavioural monitoring. Consent records and erasure proofs are kept.
  Consent requests in Arabic, separate from terms, not pre-ticked.
  (Executive Regulations Art. 14-15; PDPC consent guideline.)
- **UK**: 13 is the age for consenting alone to an online service where
  consent is the basis (UK GDPR Art. 8); school photos for administration
  rest on public task, marketing and social media on consent; withdrawn
  consent means stop and delete.
- **US**: photos where a student is the focus are education records;
  public-event photos are often directory information. COPPA (under 13):
  the vendor needs verifiable parental consent; schools may consent for
  school use only (FTC guidance; the 2025 amendments did not codify it).
- **IB**: submitted work may hold students' images and voices; the IB
  takes a licence for assessment and promotion, which a student may
  withdraw for uses outside assessment.

## 5. Egypt: duties that bite soon

The Executive Regulations (Ministerial Decision 816 of 2025) gave one year
from 1-2 November 2025 to comply, so obligations apply from about
**1-2 November 2026**:

- a PDPC licence or permit for processing (children's data being
  sensitive), decided within 90 days; schools as controllers, Farida as
  processor;
- a registered data protection officer for every legal entity;
- breach notice to the PDPC within 72 hours and to people within 3 working
  days, with a breach register;
- cross-border transfer only with a PDPC licence and consent, and the PDPC
  names cloud hosting outside Egypt as such a transfer.

These are business and legal steps for Farida and each school, not only
features; they decide where an Egyptian cell's data may live.

## 6. What a pack would set

- Retention per record type, with exam coursework held to the board's
  session dates and appeals.
- Declarations at hand-in for assessed coursework: the student's, the
  teacher's, with AI acknowledgement and evidence attached.
- A request register with each law's clock.
- Consent records with scope and expiry (Egypt), photo consent, directory
  opt-out (FERPA).
- Logs of disclosures, erasures with proof, and breaches.
