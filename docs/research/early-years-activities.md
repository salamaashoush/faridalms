# Early-years activity types (ages 3 to 7)

Scope: which interactive activities teachers of young children build, how much of the EYFS / Cambridge Early Years / PYP early years / Common Core K-2 content each covers, and which of them Farida's existing question kinds (choice, true/false, typed answer, gaps, number, matching, ordering, sorting, written answer) already express.

Method notes:

- None of the platforms publishes per-template usage totals. Wordwall's public pages give only platform totals ("120,405,921 resources created", "30 million ready-made activities", https://wordwall.net/), and the features page lists templates without counts (https://wordwall.net/en/features). Usage below is therefore a **sample**: the template type of every activity on the first results page of six early-years searches of the Wordwall community library, and the like counts Seesaw shows on its community library cards. Treat the Wordwall shares as indicative, not population figures. The tallies were extracted by an automated page reader and were not hand-checked.
- Seesaw like counts are attached to an activity, not to a grade filter (Shape Sort shows 122,035 under both PK and K), so they measure global popularity of that activity (https://app.seesaw.me/activities?grade_level=K&subject=Math, https://app.seesaw.me/activities?grade_level=Pre-K&subject=Math).
- Current EYFS: the group and school-based framework dated 13 July 2026, effective 1 September 2026 (https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5f8e4fb00f3323bf1a23a1/EYFS_group_and_school_based_from_September_2026.pdf). The ELG lines quoted below are word-for-word the same in the January 2024 edition (https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/40373/1/EYFS_statutory_framework_for_group_and_school_based_providers.pdf).

### 1b. What the frameworks ask for (the statements the activities serve)

EYFS (seven areas: three prime, communication and language, physical, PSED; four specific, literacy, mathematics, understanding the world, expressive arts and design; EYFS 2026 paras 1.4 to 1.6, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a5f8e4fb00f3323bf1a23a1/EYFS_group_and_school_based_from_September_2026.pdf):

- "The development of children's spoken language underpins all seven areas of learning and development" (same PDF, Communication and Language programme).
- ELG Word Reading: "Say a sound for each letter in the alphabet and at least 10 digraphs"; "Read words consistent with their phonic knowledge by sound-blending"; "Read aloud simple sentences and books that are consistent with their phonic knowledge".
- ELG Writing: "Write recognisable letters, most of which are correctly formed"; "Spell words by identifying sounds in them".
- ELG Comprehension: "retelling stories and narratives using their own words"; "Anticipate ... key events in stories".
- ELG Number: "Subitise (recognise quantities without counting) up to 5"; "Automatically recall ... number bonds up to 5 ... and some number bonds to 10".
- ELG Numerical Patterns: "Compare quantities up to 10"; "Explore and represent patterns within numbers up to 10".
- ELG Fine Motor: "Begin to show accuracy and care when drawing". ELG Natural World: "making observations and drawing pictures of animals and plants".
- Mathematics programme: "using manipulatives, including small pebbles and tens frames for organising counting".

Development Matters (non-statutory curriculum guidance, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64e6002a20ae890014f26cbc/DfE_Development_Matters_Report_Sep2023.pdf), reception statements that name activities directly:

- Phonological awareness: "spot and suggest rhymes; count or clap syllables in a word; recognise words with the same initial sound"; "Use magnet letters to spell a word ending like 'at'. Encourage children to put other letters in front to create rhyming words".
- "Blend sounds into words ... Ask children to work out the word you say in sounds: for example, h-a-t > hat".
- Letter formation: "the starting point and direction are more important at this stage than the size or position of the letter on a line".
- Subitise: "Show small quantities in familiar patterns (for example, dice) and random arrangements. Play games which involve quickly revealing and hiding numbers of objects. Put objects into five frames and then ten frames".
- Numeral to quantity: "Play card games such as snap or matching pairs with cards where some have numerals, and some have dot arrangements".
- Counting: "matching one number name to each item"; "Count out a smaller number from a larger group: 'Give me seven…' Knowing when to stop shows that children understand the cardinal principle".
- Patterns: "Make patterns with varying rules (including AB, ABB and ABBC) ... Make a deliberate mistake and discuss how to fix it".
- Retell: "Retell the story, once they have developed a deep familiarity with the text".
- Writing: "Apps on tablets enable children to mix marks, photos and video to express meanings and tell their own stories".

Cambridge Early Years: three stages EY1 (3 to 4), EY2 (4 to 5), EY3 (5 to 6); six areas, communication language and literacy (First- and Second-Language versions), creative expression, mathematics, PSED, physical development, understanding the world (https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/745285-cambridge-early-years-handbook-2026.pdf). Mathematics covers "developing a sense of number", "handling data", "shape, space and measure". Sample statements (https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/729772-cambridge-early-years-to-primary-transition-support-document.pdf):

- 2Mn.04 "Use ordinal numbers from 1st to 10th ... to talk about a sequence of events".
- 2Ms.09 "Copy and create simple repeating patterns ... and say what would come next in the pattern"; 3Ms.09 "identifying errors in patterns of colour, shape or size".
- 2UWd.04 "Recognise that digital devices should be used only for short periods of time".
- The FLE communication statements "can also be used as a basis for your home language curriculum" (relevant to Arabic).

IB PYP early years (3 to 6), from the IB chapter "The learner: The early learner" (© IBO 2019; ibo.org blocked automated fetch, so this is a school-hosted copy of the IB document, https://www.copenhageninternational.school/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/The-Early-Learner-IB-Primary-Years-Programme.pdf):

- Central features: "play, relationships, learning spaces, symbolic exploration and expression".
- Language and mathematics are "intentionally explored through strategies such as: games; rhymes, poems, stories; play; conversations; mark-making, drawing; problem-solving, reasoning; counting, patterning and sequencing".
- "Move away from: Literacy and numeracy experiences that develop set skills through memorization and worksheets. Move towards: ... playful, inquiry-based explorations".
- Technology: devices "could include an old camera, radio, recorder, colouring applications"; "The aim for young learners is not so much about mastering technology, but about using technology to extend their investigations through touching, seeing and hearing".

Common Core (https://www.thecorestandards.org/):

- RF.K.1.d "Recognize and name all upper- and lowercase letters"; RF.K.2.a "Recognize and produce rhyming words"; RF.K.2.b "Count, pronounce, blend, and segment syllables"; RF.K.2.c onsets and rimes; RF.K.2.d "Isolate and pronounce the initial, medial vowel, and final sounds" in CVC words; RF.K.2.e "Add or substitute individual sounds"; RF.K.3.a letter-sound correspondences; RF.K.3.c high-frequency words by sight; RF.K.4 "Read emergent-reader texts" (https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/K/). RF.1.2.b blending, RF.1.2.d "Segment spoken single-syllable words into their complete sequence of individual sounds" (https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/1/). RL.K.2 "retell familiar stories" (https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RL/K/).
- K.CC.A.3 "Write numbers from 0 to 20"; K.CC.B.4 one-to-one counting and cardinality; K.CC.B.5 "Count to answer 'how many?' questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1-20, count out that many objects"; K.CC.C.6 compare groups (https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/CC/).
- K.OA.A.1 represent with "objects, fingers, mental images, drawings, sounds (e.g., claps)"; K.OA.A.3 decompose numbers to 10 "into pairs in more than one way"; K.OA.A.4 make 10 (https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/OA/).
- K.MD.B.3 "Classify objects into given categories; count the numbers of objects in each category and sort the categories by count" (https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/MD/).
- K.G.A.1 shapes in the environment and relative position; K.G.A.2 name shapes regardless of orientation; K.G.B.5 "drawing shapes" (https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/G/).
- Number line diagrams first appear at 2.MD.B.6 (https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/MD/); EYFS guidance uses "number tracks" instead (Development Matters, above).

## Q2. Presentations of existing kinds vs genuinely new kinds

### 2a. Classification

| Activity                                                     | Existing kind it maps to                             | New interaction it needs                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Listen and choose picture                                    | Choice (single)                                      | Audio prompt with replay; picture-only options; audio on options                                                                                                          |
| Match picture-word / picture-sound / numeral-dots            | Matching                                             | Audio items (a sound matched to a picture); picture-to-picture pairs                                                                                                      |
| Memory pairs                                                 | Matching (concealed-tile presentation)               | Flip-tile presentation; score by attempts is meaningless for assessment, so practice only                                                                                 |
| Sort into groups                                             | Sorting                                              | Picture or audio items; categories headed by a picture, not text                                                                                                          |
| Sequence a story / number order                              | Ordering                                             | Picture cards laid out horizontally, flowing with document direction (RTL for Arabic)                                                                                     |
| Missing numbers                                              | Gaps or number                                       | None beyond pictures                                                                                                                                                      |
| Count how many                                               | Number (or choice of numerals)                       | Picture prompt; optional tap-to-mark each object as counted (1:1 support)                                                                                                 |
| Count out N ("give me seven")                                | **New: tappable/placeable objects**                  | Child moves or taps N objects into a container; checks the final count                                                                                                    |
| Subitising flash                                             | Choice or number                                     | Timed reveal of the prompt image (show for N seconds, then hide)                                                                                                          |
| Ten frame / number bonds                                     | Number or choice when the frame is a picture         | Building on a frame (placing counters) is the same new manipulative as count out N                                                                                        |
| Patterns: what comes next                                    | Choice (or gaps)                                     | Prompt as a row of picture tokens; options as pictures                                                                                                                    |
| Patterns: fix the mistake / odd one out                      | Choice (single)                                      | None if options can be the pattern items themselves                                                                                                                       |
| Letter-sound matching                                        | Choice or matching                                   | Teacher-recorded grapheme audio on items                                                                                                                                  |
| Rhyming, initial sound, syllable count                       | Choice, matching, sorting, number                    | Audio on pictures (these are spoken-word tasks)                                                                                                                           |
| Build a word from letter tiles                               | **New: word builder** (closest existing is ordering) | Tile bank of graphemes, including multi-letter graphemes as one tile, plus distractors; slot count per sound; target word as audio; Arabic tiles must join when assembled |
| Tracing letters / numbers                                    | **New: tracing**                                     | Ink capture with a stroke model (start point, direction, order) checked per stroke, following the DM rule that start and direction matter more than size                  |
| Drawing / colouring                                          | **New: drawing**                                     | Free ink canvas over an optional background image; colouring is the same block with fill; teacher-reviewed                                                                |

### 2b. What the existing kinds are missing for this age group

These gaps block most of the ranked list even where the kind already exists:

1. **Audio on the prompt, with replay and optional autoplay.** Pre-readers cannot read instructions; Seesaw's guidance is to add voice to all directions so children work independently (https://www.kindergartencafe.org/5-steps-to-making-an-interactive-seesaw-activity/), and Boom adds audio "to help young learners or learners who are not at the reading level" (https://missjennyot.com/what-are-boom-cards-and-how-do-they-work/).
2. **Audio on every option, item and category** of choice, matching, sorting and ordering. Rhyme, initial sound and letter-sound tasks are about sounds, not text (RF.K.2, https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/K/; DM phonological awareness, https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64e6002a20ae890014f26cbc/DfE_Development_Matters_Report_Sep2023.pdf).
3. **Picture-only options and categories with large touch targets.** Boom ships "multiple pictures" as a premade answer block (https://helpcenter.boomlearning.com/what-answer-types-are-there-does-every-card-need-to-have-a-question).
4. **Timed reveal** of a prompt image for subitising ("quickly revealing and hiding", Development Matters).
5. **Ordering laid out horizontally in document direction**, with picture cards.
6. **Distractors and grapheme units** in a tile bank (word builder).
7. **Manipulatives**: tap each object to count, drag N objects into a box or onto a ten frame (Khan Kids, https://blog.khanacademy.org/khan-kids-kindergarten-math-games; K.CC.B.5 "count out that many objects", https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/CC/).
8. **Hotspots on an image.**
9. **Ink capture**, both free (drawing) and stroke-checked (tracing).
10. **Media capture answers**: voice, photo, video, reviewed by the teacher rather than auto-marked. Most of Seesaw's top early-years activities end in a recording that the teacher reviews (https://app.seesaw.me/activities/r66ekn/digraph-word-sort).
11. **A lesson card with no question** (Boom "lesson cards", same Boom help page), which Farida's text, picture and audio blocks already cover.

Not researched here (left to other sections or unverified): evidence on finger vs stylus tracing on tablets, Arabic-specific tile joining rules, and screen-time limits.
