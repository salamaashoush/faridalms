Written 2026-10-01 as the follow-up to [coursework.md](coursework.md) and
[course-design.md](course-design.md), which it does not repeat. Section 1
covers assignments, section 2 interactive courses, section 3 the decisions

## 1. Assignments

### 1.1 Assignment types

Almost every "type" is a starting artefact each student gets a copy of, some
machine-checkable interactions, and voice, photo or drawing capture.

- **Classroom practice sets** (GC/13455315, WU/2023/04/practice-sets-for-google-classroom.html):
  short answer, paragraph, single and multiple select, a maths keyboard,
  images, hints or videos as "Extra Help", skill tags driving suggested
  resources, "allow equivalent answers" and "ignore capitalization". Students
  see right or wrong at once. English only, paid tier.
- **Interactive YouTube questions** (GC/14010140, GC/14009444): questions at
  timestamps pause the video; students retry until right, then Turn in.
- **Edpuzzle** (support.edpuzzle.com/hc/en-us/articles/360007745911): Prevent
  Skipping (back but not past the furthest point), attempts, answers shown only
  on the last attempt, paste blocked on open answers, per-teacher default
  settings.
- **Make a copy for each student** (GC/6020265,
  AK/2015/07/20/google-classroom-make-a-copy-for-each-student/): view, edit or
  copy. Traps: the mode is fixed once posted, template edits never reach copies
  already made, and copies are made lazily (AK/2020/04/07/nocopies/). Teams
  limits "Students edit their own copy" to Office files
  (MS/assignments/create-an-assignment-in-microsoft-teams).
- **Toddle workbook template** (help.toddleapp.com/en/articles/9853270): each
  student marks up their own copy with text, handwriting, highlight, image,
  audio and video; teachers annotate live and reply by video or audio.
- **Book Creator** (BC/8587478, BC/6459601): assigned to the whole library by
  default including later joiners; page lock; text, audio or video comments.
- **Canvas Student Annotation** (CV/661221): a PDF or Word template the student
  annotates as a layer; marked in SpeedGrader; no groups or peer review.
- **Kami** (KM/8573672, KM/9574560): copy per student by default, autograded
  questions placed on a PDF, Class View of every file live, voice and video
  comments, an annotation bank.
- **Seesaw** (seesaw.com/blog/whats-new-in-seesaw/): multi-page templates with
  locked items, draw-while-recording, Focus Mode, Read-with-Me highlighting, a
  reading-fluency tool (accuracy, words correct per minute).
- **Formative** (FM/6198532, FM/8206965): "Show your work" whiteboard on any
  question, a live colour-coded response grid, a per-question gallery across
  students with group scoring, the last three feedback messages as quick picks,
  score release instantly, when closed or after submitting.
- **Teams Reading Progress** (MS/learning-accelerators/getting-started-with-reading-progress-in-teams):
  passage, attempts, pronunciation sensitivity, video or audio; auto-detected
  errors the teacher corrects into accuracy and words correct per minute;
  Microsoft warns detection "may not recognize accents and dialects well".
  Speaker Progress and Math Progress (2024, 2025 Microsoft Education blog)
  follow the same shape; Math Progress adds misconception-based distractors and
  a photo of the working.
- **Read Along in Classroom** (WU/2026/06/read-along-in-google-classroom-is-now-available-to-all-education-users-to-support-foundational-literacy.html):
  free from July 2026, libraries in eight languages including Arabic.
- **Homework from live tools**: Nearpod student-paced codes up to 365 days;
  Pear Deck Takeaways, a per-student document of slides and answers for
  teacher comments (help.peardeck.com).
- **Teams 2026**: matching, fill in the blank and flashcards inside
  assignments; per-assignment AI-use guidelines that inform but do not enforce
  (MS/assignments/set-ai-guidelines-assignment-microsoft-teams).

### 1.2 The student work surface and feedback

- Classroom's grading tool (GC/9093530): inline comments on a selection, a
  comment bank opened with "#", recorded audio, video or screen feedback up to
  five minutes. Trap: private comments cannot be edited or deleted.
- Teams (MS/assignments/grade-return-and-reassign-assignments): arrows between
  students, Return, Return for revision, Excuse and return, bulk return, video
  feedback, and handing in on a student's behalf for offline work.
- Canvas's comment library is per teacher and plain text.
- EEF: plan how pupils receive and use feedback and give time to act on it;
  verbal feedback may be more time-efficient
  (educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk feedback guidance report). Homework is
  worth about five months in secondary and two in primary, with quality and
  feedback mattering more than quantity (EEF toolkit, homework).

### 1.3 Differentiation

- Teams "Extend due date" for one or more students, from the assignment or
  while returning work; the date reaches the student's list, digest and
  gradebook. Classroom still has none (AK/2017/02/26/google-classroom-extend-due-date/);
  its student groups are teacher-only and sortable in Student work
  (WU/2025/04/new-student-group-capabilities-google-classroom.html).
- Canvas New Quizzes accommodations are set per student per course: extra
  time, one fewer wrong option, extra attempts; the 2025 tray warns, without
  blocking, when extra time runs past the due date
  (developerdocs.instructure.com/services/canvas/resources/new_quizzes_accommodations).
- Formative turns text to speech on per student once (FM/6203043); Teams
  Immersive Reader reads or translates assignment text in place.
- Pitfalls: Canvas Student View cannot preview differentiated work; Canvas
  group rubric feedback is all-group or all-individual.

### 1.4 Reuse and families

- Classroom Reuse post copies attachments into a mess of duplicates
  (AK/2019/08/06/caution-copying-google-classroom-makes-a-giant-mess-of-your-google-drive/);
  Canvas Copy To and Send To (CV/660686, CV/660687); Schoology Save to
  Resources; school template libraries in Toddle.
- Families: Classroom guardian preview shows classwork but no grades
  (WU/2024/07/enable-guardians-to-preview-assigned-classwork-google-calendar.html);
  Canvas Parent alerts on grade thresholds and missing work; Seesaw and
  ClassDojo show only teacher-approved work; Toddle shows task, submission and
  evaluation status. Classroom's AI and practice sets are English only, so
  Arabic support is open ground.

### 1.5 Recommendations for assignments

1. T1 **A starting document per student**: a template part of the brief is
   copied into each student's response the first time they open it, with
   lockable teacher blocks; Update reaches copies not yet touched. Extends the
   brief document, the online and both formats, and held Update.
2. T1 **Extend due date** for chosen students from Student work and from
   Return for revision, shown in their To do and the family page. Extends
   `assignment_recipients.due_at`, which already exists for this.
3. T1 **Inline comments in the student's response and a per-teacher comment
   bank** opened with "#", in both languages, editable until Return.
4. T1 **Recorded voice feedback and voice instructions** on the brief.
5. T1 **Grading one question across the class** with bulk score and feedback.
6. T1 **Hand in for a student** for work on paper.
7. T2 **Accommodations per student**, set once: extra quiz time, read-aloud,
   fewer choices, default extension days; warn rather than block past due.
8. T2 **Practice mode for quizzes**: right or wrong at once, hints, retries.
9. T2 **Interactive video preset**: media with checks at timestamps and no
   skipping.
10. T2 **Show your work**: photo, audio, video and drawing in the response.
11. T2 **Use again** from any class or year, personal and school libraries,
    resources referenced rather than copied.
12. T2 **Families see returned feedback and handed-in work**, with alerts and
    a digest in the parent's language.
13. T2 **Read aloud and translate the brief in place.**
14. T3 **Reading fluency assignments** with teacher-marked errors first and
    cautious automatic detection for Egyptian and Gulf speech.
15. T3 **AI-use guideline and skill tags** under More options.

## 2. Interactive courses

Help centres for Khan Academy, CK-12, Seesaw, Learning A-Z and Nearpod
refused automated reads; claims marked _(excerpt)_ rest on search snippets.

### 2.1 Product mechanics

- **Brilliant**: the puzzle comes first and the name after (balance scales
  before algebra, https://blog.brilliant.org/solving-equations/); a wrong
  answer is "try again" with no penalty; each lesson's practice removes the
  visual aids, and review sets mix earlier lessons
  (https://brilliant.org/about/).
- **Khan Academy** 2025-26: Unit Missions replaced mastery goals; progress
  reads "N of M skills Proficient" and a learner queue
  (https://blog.khanacademy.org/why-khan-academy-will-be-using-skills-to-proficient-to-measure-learning-outcomes/).
  A hint in practice marks the item wrong; in quizzes hints come only after a
  wrong answer _(excerpt)_.
- **Duolingo**: missed exercises are asked again at the end of the lesson
  (https://blog.duolingo.com/spaced-repetition-for-learning/); a perfect run
  swaps the last items for harder ones; hearts gave way to energy because
  beginners ran out mid-lesson twice as often
  (https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy/); explanations are offered on
  right answers too.
- **Desmos, now Amplify Classroom**: the teacher paces students to a range of
  screens, pauses the class, anonymises names, and presents chosen student
  responses as snapshots
  (https://blog.desmos.com/articles/orchestrate-more-productive-mathematics/).
  The dashboard shows each student per screen in one of five states: correct,
  wrong, warning, needs a human, nothing to do. Feedback is deliberately held
  until submit during concept building
  (https://blog.mrmeyer.com/2017/desmos-design-why-were-suspicious-of-immediate-feedback/).
- **Wayground presentations**: student-paced up to a chosen slide, a Classroom
  Pulse of low-accuracy students, a mastery goal of 80, 90 or 100 percent and
  redemption questions at the end
  (https://help.wayground.com/support/solutions/articles/158000404930).
- **Nearpod**: a red dot on students not taking part, one answer shared
  anonymously, generated questions the teacher picks from
  (https://nearpod.com/blog/nearpod-updates/).
- **CK-12**: questions inside a FlexBook do not reach reports _(excerpt)_, the
  gap to avoid.
- **Century Tech**: worked example, "your turn", then independent questions,
  with written explanations on misconception distractors; an interventions
  view of time against score in four quadrants
  (https://www.century.tech/news/the-science-behind-century/).
- **Kognity**: results group wrong answers by how often each was chosen; a
  strength bar decays after about a month without testing.
- **Arabic-region platforms** (Alef, Abjadiyat, Abwaab, Noon, I Read Arabic):
  coins, leagues, live classes, recorded reading for the teacher; none
  documents its feedback design, and Alef's outcome claims are not
  peer-reviewed.

### 2.2 Structure, pacing and teacher views

- **Mastery learning** (EEF toolkit): +5 months, low security, +8 primary and
  +3 secondary; it needs a high bar (80 to 90 percent) and is "much less
  effective when pupils work at their own pace"
  (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning).
  This favours teacher-paced unit checkpoints over self-paced looping.
- **Mixed review**: interleaved grade 7 practice scored 61 against 38 percent
  a month later, d = 0.83 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1237752); the IES 2021
  maths guide (Strong) mixes earlier and new material within and across
  lessons and fades worked examples
  (https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/WWC2021006-Math-PG.pdf).
- **A live teacher view helps**: Lumilo (286 students) raised learning, more
  for students who started low; its alerts were idle two minutes or more,
  rapid guessing, hint abuse or avoidance, and still wrong after every hint;
  teachers asked for one symbol per student, positive alerts too, and alerts
  kept private
  (https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bmclaren/pubs/HolsteinMcLarenAleven-StudentLearningBenefitsMixedRealityTeacherAwarnessTool-AIED2018.pdf).
- **ASSISTments** (immediate feedback, hints, a teacher report): g = 0.18 in
  Maine and +0.10 in the North Carolina replication, low prior attainers
  gaining most.

### 2.3 Feedback

- EEF feedback: +7 months primary, +5 secondary, verbal +7, digital only +4;
  feedback on correct work matters too
  (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback).
  The platform's job is to route errors to the teacher, not to replace them.
- Timing: a 2026 meta-analysis of 51 studies found immediate against delayed
  g = 0.03 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-026-10117-8), so
  timing is a setting, not a doctrine.
- Try again loops (Attali 2015): several tries then the answer beat showing
  the answer at once, and tries with hints did better still, more so for open
  items (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131515300312).
  Matific runs retry, then an illustrated hint, then worked steps.
- Elaborated feedback beats right or wrong alone
  (https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314564881).
- Misconception distractors (Eedi): each wrong option maps to a
  misconception, two attempts before support, reports grouping students by the
  option chosen; +2 to +4 months in a vendor-published trial.
- Problem first: productive failure g = 0.36, up to 0.58 when designed
  faithfully
  (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3102/00346543211019105);
  prequestions g = 0.66 for the targeted idea and nothing for the rest
  (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10075-7).
- Worked examples in maths g = 0.48, correct examples alone working best
  (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1364058).

### 2.4 Young children

- Khan Kids reads any text when tapped and celebrates only a correct first
  try. Lalilo colours each lesson green above 80 percent, orange below and red
  below 50, reopening red lessons; when its speech recognition is unsure it
  shows the answer rather than marking the child wrong
  (https://intercom.help/lalilo/en/articles/5186626-read-aloud-exercises).
- Touch at three to six: taps miss the centre by 4.5 mm at age three, presses
  up to five seconds should count as taps, multi-touch drags succeed about
  half the time (https://mintviz.usv.ro/publications/ijhcs2015.pdf).
- Rewards: a promised reward lowered preschoolers' interest, an unexpected
  one did not; process praise beats person praise; stars covering the screen
  and tokens on every action rated worse.
- Story apps: narration helps, hotspots and games distract
  (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1081714).
- Timed fluency practice as "meet or beat" your own score is rated Strong for
  struggling students (IES 2021, recommendation 6), which qualifies a blanket
  "no timers".

### 2.5 Arabic

- Measured in Chrome 153: an input gap inside an Arabic word breaks letter
  joining; U+200D on both sides restores it; inline-block chips break joining
  too, coloured inline spans do not. Gaps, letter tiles and highlighted letters
  need joiner-aware rendering, and a built word must be redrawn as one run.
- About 97 percent of Arabic text omits harakat; unvowelled text arrives
  around grade 4; wrong harakat are worse than none; neural diacritisers
  mis-vowel 8.6 to 10.9 percent of words, so speech synthesis of unvowelled
  text is unsafe for young readers.
- Egypt EGRA 2014, grade 3: 27.3 percent read no words; the letter test shows
  letters with harakat in all four positional forms. Wrong positional forms
  and dot confusions (ب ت ث) are leading early errors; tracing runs right to
  left with dots last.
- Children of nine to twelve read faster in 14pt simplified Arabic than in
  Traditional Arabic (http://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/16427/).

### 2.6 Authoring burden

Teachers spend about seven hours a week finding materials and five making
them (https://edreports.org/impact/why-materials-matter); weekly AI users
save 5.9 hours (Gallup 2025). Wayground generates from PDF, Word, slides, a
photo of a worksheet or a video. Teacher-edited AI items carried more flaws
than teacher-written ones (4.87 against 2.70 per item), read as automation
bias (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2026.1831250/full),
so generated items need a review gate.

## 3. Decisions for Farida

What changes in the plan, in the order the work lands:

1. **Assignments now** (in progress): DocumentPage editor, held Update for a
   live brief, readers as pages. Then, in this order: extend due date per
   student (`assignment_recipients.due_at` exists), hand in for a student,
   inline comments and a comment bank, voice feedback, a starting document per
   student.
2. **Question blocks in the editor** follow
   [editor-question-blocks.md](editor-question-blocks.md): ElementNode trees
   with NodeState settings, a question mode in the one floating bar, ids kept
   as UUIDs, the server deriving `LessonBlock`s, a permissive schema with
   publish checks. Quizzes and lessons move onto them together.
3. **Every check gains** a misconception note per wrong option, a help ladder
   (try again, hint, worked steps) recording first-try correctness, a
   feedback timing setting, and end-of-lesson redemption of missed checks.
4. **Teacher views**: a who-chose-what grid per check and a class progress grid
   in the five Desmos states, private to the teacher; live alerts later.
5. **Arabic as a first-class concern**: joiner-aware gaps and tiles, a
   harakat setting per document, an Arabic type scale.
6. **Young children**: audio on prompts and options, large hit areas
   accepting long presses, no multi-touch, unexpected celebrations only.
7. **Next block kinds**: mixed review built from earlier checks, worked
   example with fading, predict before reveal, unit checkpoints shown as
   "N of M skills proficient".
8. **Authoring aids** after the above: suggested checks from a document with
   an unreviewed state that blocks publishing, a school library with copy and
   edit, skill tags rolling up to unit coverage.

## 4. Files in documents

Researched 2026-10-01 when the assignment page still had an attachment list
beside its brief.

- Document tools put files in the body: Notion's file and PDF blocks
  (https://www.notion.com/help/images-files-and-media), Confluence's inline
  file tiles with preview, download and copy
  (https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/display-files-and-images/),
  Linear and GitHub, where an upload becomes part of the text
  (https://docs.github.com/en/get-started/writing-on-github/working-with-advanced-formatting/attaching-files).
  Canvas links files inside rich content with an inline or overlay preview.
- Classroom and Teams keep a separate list only because each item behaves
  differently per student (a copy for each, edit rights) and their
  instructions cannot hold files
  (https://developers.google.com/workspace/classroom/reference/rest/v1/Material,
  https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/educationassignmentresource).
- A separate list that is its own data drifts: Confluence's attachment list
  keeps files the page no longer shows
  (https://support.atlassian.com/confluence-cloud/docs/manage-uploaded-files/).
  Jira's panel is a view over one store, and files are shared, not copied.
- Readers weigh a file before opening it: GOV.UK shows "PDF, 19.5 KB, 7
  pages" with one focusable link
  (https://components.publishing.service.gov.uk/component-guide/attachment);
  Moodle shows size and type by default for slow connections
  (https://docs.moodle.org/en/File_resource_settings); WebAIM puts the type
  in the link text; Latin names in Arabic text are isolated with `bdi`.

What Farida does: files are blocks in the document only; `document_files`
is the index rebuilt from the content on every save and keeps files the
published copy still shows; whoever may read a document may open its files,
and saving refuses a file the author could not open. Files are served inline
for viewing with byte ranges, and download on request. The block shows type
and size, opens in a viewer (pages, pictures, sound, video), can show a PDF's
pages in place, and is downloaded or linked from the floating bar. Uploads
show progress and the reason for a refusal, and publishing waits for them.
A copy per student, when it comes, is a setting on the file block.
